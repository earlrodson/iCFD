● A Case for You to Test — No Faith Required to Start

You Don't Have to Believe Anything Yet. Just Look at the Evidence.

This isn't "the Bible says it, so it's true." It's five separate claims, in order — each argued from evidence you can check without first accepting Christianity, and each one only appears once the last has actually earned it.

"You're not being asked to assume Christianity is true. Establish the historical data first — then judge for yourself which explanation actually fits it."

How this worksEvery conclusion has to be earned by its own evidence — none of them get to borrow credibility from the next.
The bar it has to clearNot certainty — is the evidence strong enough that denying it becomes unreasonable?
What you won't find hereCircular reasoning. If a claim can't stand without assuming the conclusion, it's flagged as such.
00 — Structure

The Flow: Five Separate Claims, Not One

Each row is its own question with its own evidence. You're free to accept, say, that Jesus existed while rejecting that he rose — that's a coherent position, not a contradiction. If you find yourself rejecting a later row, it's worth checking whether your objection is actually about that row, or is really an objection to an earlier one you already granted.

PhaseCore QuestionMain EvidenceConclusion
1 · Jesus Historical Did Jesus actually exist? Paul, Gospels, Josephus, Tacitus, archaeology, historical method Jesus of Nazareth existed
2 · Bible as Source Can we trust the Bible as historical evidence? Dating, authorship, eyewitness access, manuscripts, external corroboration A legitimate, substantially reliable source
3 · God Exists Why does anything exist, and what explains reality? Contingency, cosmological argument, fine-tuning, morality, consciousness, reason God's existence is rationally justified
4 · Resurrection What best explains the Resurrection evidence? Crucifixion, 1 Cor 15, Peter, James, Paul, empty tomb, alternative explanations Bodily Resurrection is the best explanation
5 · Jesus Is God If Jesus rose, who did God vindicate? Jesus' claims, divine prerogatives, Son of Man, worship, Resurrection, Jewish monotheism Jesus' divinity is defensible
Why order matters: "Jesus existed" is a historical question. "Jesus rose" is historical and philosophical. "Jesus is God" is theological. Each needs its own argument — granting Phase 1 commits you to nothing about Phase 4. Nobody's tracking whether you agree; this is just where the disagreement should actually live.
01 — Foundation

Did Jesus Exist?

Start with sources that don't require accepting Christianity: a hostile Roman historian, a Jewish historian with no evangelistic motive, and Paul's own letters connecting him to Jesus' inner circle within two decades of the crucifixion.

Verdict: extraordinarily well established by ordinary ancient-historical standards

Josephus on James Very strong

Calls James "the brother of Jesus who was called Christ." A Jewish historian with no interest in promoting Christianity treats Jesus as an ordinary historical reference point.

Antiquities of the Jews, Bk. 20 · c. AD 93–94

Tacitus Very strong

Explains Nero's persecution by noting Christians derive their name from "Christus," executed under Pontius Pilate during Tiberius' reign. Tacitus was hostile to the movement, not promoting it.

Annals 15.44 · c. AD 116

Paul's letters Very strong

Seven undisputed letters, dated to the AD 50s — roughly two decades after the crucifixion. Paul personally met Peter and James in Jerusalem (Gal. 1:18–19).

Galatians, 1 Corinthians, Romans…

The Pilate Stone Context

An inscription at Caesarea confirms Pilate's office. It doesn't prove Jesus existed — it confirms the political setting the Gospels describe is real, not invented.

Archaeological, Caesarea Maritima

Pliny the Younger Moderate

Writing to Trajan c. AD 111–113, describes an established movement already singing hymns to Christ "as to a god" — evidence of scale, not biography.

Letters 10.96

The crucifixion itself Very strong

Not an obvious invention for a Jewish messianic movement — a crucified messiah was a theological embarrassment, which is why it's such strong evidence.

The Socrates test. Socrates wrote nothing, appears differently across sources, and has no modern documentary record — yet no historian doubts he existed. The fair question for a skeptic: "What standard are you using to reject Jesus, and would you apply it to Socrates?" If the answer is "Jesus made supernatural claims," that's a reason to scrutinize the resurrection claim later — not a reason to doubt the man existed.
02 — Sources

Can We Trust the Bible as Evidence?

An atheist doesn't need to believe the Bible is inspired to treat it as a historical document worth examining — the same way historians use Herodotus or Plutarch despite their biases.

Verdict: a legitimate historical source, examined the same way any ancient text is examined

Ten historical criteria applied to the text

  • Chronological proximity — how close is the source to the events?
  • Eyewitness access — Paul met Peter and James directly (Gal. 1:18–19)
  • Earlier traditions preserved in later texts — the 1 Cor. 15 creed predates the letter itself
  • Multiple attestation — the same fact appears in independent traditions
  • External corroboration — Josephus, Tacitus, Pliny
  • Archaeological corroboration — Pilate, Nazareth, Capernaum, crucifixion practice
  • Embarrassing details — women as first witnesses; Peter's denial; disciples' confusion
  • Enemy attestation — even the "disciples stole the body" polemic confirms the tomb was known and empty

What you can grant without becoming a Christian

You can accept every item below and still reject Christianity — that's a genuinely consistent position, not a trick:

  • Jesus existed and was crucified
  • Paul existed and knew Peter and James
  • Early Christians sincerely believed Jesus appeared to them
  • Christianity began in the first century, not centuries later
If you grant all four, your disagreement isn't over these facts — it's over what best explains them. That's a sharper, more useful place to disagree than "is the text true."

03 — Philosophy

Does God Exist?

This phase runs on philosophy, not the Bible — it has to, or the whole case becomes circular. The target isn't "the Catholic God" yet, just a necessary, transcendent ground of contingent reality.

Verdict: theism is a serious, rationally defensible position — not the default forced by science
ArgumentQuestion it answersTheistic implication
ContingencyWhy does anything contingent exist?A necessary ground
Kalam cosmologicalWhy does the universe exist / begin?A transcendent cause
IntelligibilityWhy is reality mathematically knowable?A rational source
Fine-tuningWhy life-permitting constants?Possible design
Objective moralityWhy are some acts wrong regardless of opinion?A moral foundation
ConsciousnessWhy does subjective experience exist?Mind as fundamental
?

"If God made the universe, who made God?"

The argument never claimed everything needs a cause — only contingent things do. God is proposed as necessary, not contingent. Ask back: what's your candidate for the necessary thing — brute-fact universe, multiverse, laws of physics — and why is it necessary rather than just unexplained?

?

"A multiverse explains fine-tuning."

Maybe — but it doesn't remove the ultimate-explanation question, it relocates it: why does the multiverse exist, and why does it run on laws that generate universes at all?

?

"This is a God-of-the-gaps argument."

Not the same move. "Science can't yet explain X, so God" is weak. "What grounds contingent existence itself" is a question science was never built to answer — it's not a gap, it's outside the method's domain.

?

"Physicists don't all agree the universe 'began.'"

Correct, and worth saying plainly: eternal inflation, cyclic models, and boundary-condition cosmologies are live alternatives, and Kalam's second premise is genuinely more contested among cosmologists than its first. That's why this case leans harder on the contingency argument above it, which doesn't need the universe to have had a beginning — only that its existence, however old, doesn't explain itself.

?

"If God exists, why is he so silent — why does he allow suffering?"

The problem of evil isn't answered anywhere on this page, and it shouldn't be waved off as if it were. It's a genuine counter-consideration to theism in general, argued about for centuries, and it sits outside what contingency, cosmology, and fine-tuning are built to address. A complete case for God has to engage it eventually — this phase only argues that a necessary transcendent ground is rationally defensible, not that every question about that ground's character is settled.

If you've heard weaker versions of this argument before, here's what's not being claimed on this page:

Not claimed"The Big Bang proves Genesis."
Not claimed"DNA is complex, therefore God."
Not claimed"Quantum physics proves God."
Not claimed"Science can't disprove God, therefore God exists."
04 — The Hinge

The Resurrection: What Best Explains the Evidence?

This is where the case is won or lost. Not "the Bible says he rose" — a set of historical facts that any explanation, naturalistic or not, has to account for in full.

Verdict: bodily Resurrection is the strongest explanation of the total evidence, especially once Phase 3 makes it non-impossible

Very strongly established

  • Jesus existed and was crucified under Pilate
  • The earliest Christians proclaimed he'd been raised — within the first generation
  • Paul converted after opposing the movement, citing a personal encounter
  • James, Jesus' own brother, became a Jerusalem church leader
  • 1 Cor. 15:3–8 preserves a creed older than the letter itself

Strong but more contested

  • Jesus was buried in a known tomb
  • The tomb was found empty
Keeping these two tiers separate is what makes the argument hard to attack — it never claims more certainty than the evidence supports. And the tomb is the weaker of the two for a specific reason: no non-Christian source corroborates it. Josephus and Tacitus confirm the crucifixion; nothing outside the Christian tradition confirms the burial or the empty tomb. It rests on the Gospel account plus the inference that hostile authorities could have simply produced the body and didn't — suggestive, not decisive on its own.

The honest concession: martyrdom proves sincerity, not truth. People die for false beliefs. What it establishes is narrower and still useful — Peter, James, and Paul weren't reporting inherited folklore, they were reporting what they said was personal experience, and they held to it under real cost.

Two questions the evidence alone can't settle

?

"Couldn't you just say 'unknown natural cause' at every step instead of resurrection?"

Yes — and that's a stable position, not a dodge. Comparing explanations only compels someone who already treats a supernatural cause as a candidate worth weighing against natural ones. Rule that out beforehand and no amount of convergence in the table above will move the conclusion, because the evidence was never what was doing the work. What this table actually shows is narrower: the Resurrection is rationally permissible given the evidence, not that it's forced on anyone who looks at it.

?

"Isn't testimony for a miracle always less probable than the testimony simply being mistaken?" (Hume's argument)

This is a real, unresolved philosophical dispute, not something this table settles — it relocates it. Whether that's true depends entirely on how plausible a cause capable of resurrection is in the first place, which is exactly what Phase 3 was for. Find Hume's argument persuasive independent of whether God exists, and nothing in this table is likely to change your mind — which is worth admitting rather than talking past.

05 — Implication

If He Rose, Who Was Vindicated?

This phase only opens once Phase 4 is granted — it's a conclusion, not a starting assumption. If the Resurrection is the best explanation of the evidence, the next honest question is what it implies about the man it happened to.

Status: the logical next step of the case — argued from Jesus' own claims and earliest worship, not asserted
Why this phase has to carry real weight: Phase 3 only established a necessary, transcendent, plausibly intelligent ground of reality — not a personal God who reveals himself in history, and not specifically the Christian one. That's the largest structural gap in the whole case, and it isn't closed by Phase 3 at all. It's closed here, or not at all: if the Resurrection didn't happen, nothing stops that necessary ground from being impersonal, deistic, or claimed by another tradition instead.

His claims

Forgiving sins, claiming authority over the Sabbath, and using "Son of Man" language drawn from Daniel 7 — claims that made sense as blasphemy to first-century Jewish monotheists precisely because they weren't ordinary.

Earliest worship

Philippians 2 and Colossians 1 — texts scholars date remarkably early — already describe Jesus in language of divine worship, within a community that came from strict Jewish monotheism.

The vindication logic

A resurrection, if real, is God acting in history. The question becomes: if God raised this specific man, making these specific claims, what was God vindicating?

06 — Reference

Your Objections, Answered Honestly

These are the strongest pushbacks — the ones worth actually raising, not straw men. Where the honest answer requires conceding a point, it's conceded here rather than argued around.

?

"The Gospels are biased — they're written by believers."

Bias isn't the same as fabrication. Tacitus, Josephus, and Herodotus all wrote with agendas, and historians still extract reliable information from them. The question is never "does this source have a worldview" — it's what can be recovered from it despite one.

?

"The Bible's been copied and changed too many times to trust."

Textual criticism exists because manuscripts differ — that's not a scandal, it's the method. Thousands of witnesses let scholars compare readings and flag variants; most are spelling or word order. The claim isn't "we have the originals," it's "we can reconstruct the earliest attainable text with high confidence."

?

"Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence."

Fair — and worth separating what needs which standard. "Jesus existed" needs ordinary ancient-historical evidence, which it has in abundance. "Jesus rose" is the extraordinary claim, and it's argued on its own, later, against explicit competing hypotheses — not smuggled in on the coattails of the easier claim.

?

"Miracles violate the laws of science."

Science describes regular natural processes; it doesn't rule on whether something outside nature acted once. That's a metaphysical claim about whether nature is all there is — which is exactly what Phase 3 exists to test, not something to assume going in.

?

"Every apostle died for this, so it must be true."

Too strong, and worth dropping. Martyrdom shows sincerity, not accuracy — and the manner of death for several apostles is itself under-documented. Use the narrower, defensible version: people claiming personal experience, not inherited belief, held to it under real cost.

?

"Other religions have martyrs and miracle claims too — why doesn't this method 'prove' those as well?"

Fair, and it deserves a real answer rather than a dismissal. What's being claimed as unusual here is a specific combination: a skeptical relative's conversion (James), a former persecutor's conversion (Paul), corroboration from hostile non-Christian historians, and a proclamation appearing within years rather than generations. Whether that combination is actually rarer than comparable claims elsewhere is something you're entitled to test against specific comparisons — it isn't established just by asserting it here.

07 — Walk It Through

The Case, Claim by Claim

Same argument as above, broken into single steps. Test each one on its own before moving to the next — most people find they agree with more of this list than they expected, and the real disagreement turns out to live in just one or two places.

Do you agree Jesus existed?Ordinary ancient-historical standard — Josephus, Tacitus, Paul.
Do you agree he was crucified?One of the best-attested facts about him — Christian and non-Christian sources converge.
Do you agree his earliest followers believed they encountered him alive afterward?Distinct from "he actually rose" — this is just about sincere, very early belief.
Do you agree Paul converted after claiming an encounter with Jesus, and knew Peter and James personally?Rules out "invented generations later, by strangers to the movement."
Do you agree James, Jesus' own brother, became a leader of the movement?A skeptical family member becoming a believer needs its own explanation.
Given all of that — what explanation accounts for the whole set of facts, not just part of it?This is where the real disagreement lives. Everything above it is meant to already be common ground.
If you stop and disagree at any stage, the case doesn't collapse — it just narrows. Disagreeing at stage 3, for instance, still leaves stages 1–2 standing on their own historical merits; it just means the conversation from there on should be about resurrection appearances specifically, not about whether Jesus existed. Locating exactly where you get off is more useful than a blanket "I don't buy it."
Two things worth noticing about how this was built: nowhere above does it say "the Bible says it, therefore it's true" — it starts only from what you already have independent reason to accept. And it doesn't inflate certainty to win a point: "the tomb was empty" is flagged as more contested than "Jesus was crucified," rather than being quietly treated the same.
08 — Summary

If You Only Read One Paragraph

Here's the whole case compressed to what matters.

Jesus unquestionably existed and was crucified. His earliest followers, within the first generation, sincerely proclaimed he had risen — a claim Paul and James each attached to a personal encounter, not inherited folklore. Every naturalistic alternative explains part of that picture; none explains all of it without piling on independent, unproven assumptions. If God exists — which Phase 3 argues on its own terms — a resurrection stops being metaphysically impossible and becomes a historical hypothesis worth weighing on the evidence.

You don't have to walk away agreeing with any of it. The narrower claim being made is that this explanation is intellectually and historically defensible — and that whatever explanation you land on instead has to answer to the same evidence you just read, not to less of it.
